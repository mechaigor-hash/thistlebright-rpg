A fairy-tale tabletop RPG for brave 5–7 year olds

Part 1
Making a Hero

Welcome to the glen

Every adventure begins with a tiny brave choice. The Guide reads the scene, the players say what they try, and the dice help everyone discover what happens next.

2

The table rule

Adventures in Alba is played by talking together. A grown-up Guide describes a place, then each Adventurer says what their hero tries. The rules stay small so the story can stay big.

Read aloud: “You hear bells under the heather. A fox with silver whiskers bows and waits. What do you do?”

When to roll

Only roll when the answer is exciting. If an idea is safe and simple, it works. If it is tricky, roll one six-sided die.

d6 + statWhat happens
1–3A wobble: something funny or inconvenient happens.
4–5A yes, but: success with a tiny cost or choice.
6+A bright success: the hero does it well.
Kindness rule: heroes can be scared, surprised, muddy, or silly, but the story never punishes an Adventurer for trying to help.

The five stats

  • Strength for lifting, holding, and protecting.
  • Wis for noticing, comforting, and understanding.
  • Agility for sneaking, catching, and balancing. Add Int for puzzles and Luck for fairy chances.

Step 1: Pick your kindred

Your kindred is your fairy-tale people. It gives one story gift and a way to picture your hero. Nobody is better or worse because of their kindred.

4

Kindreds of Alba

Glenfolk

Practical adventurers from cottages, crofts, and market lanes.

  • Gift: once per adventure, remember a useful local clue.
  • Look: tartan scarf, muddy boots, treasure pockets.

Thistle Fairy

Small bright folk with shimmer, manners, and secret paths.

  • Gift: once per scene, notice nearby fairy magic.
  • Look: petal cloak, star freckles, tiny crown.

Brownie Helper

Cozy fixers who tidy, mend, and improve small things.

  • Gift: repair or improve one tiny object each scene.
  • Look: apron, tool pouch, flour on nose.

Selkie-Born

Gentle loch-hearted heroes with moonlit dreams.

  • Gift: understand water, weather, or a sad feeling.
  • Look: soft seal-cloak, shell button, sea-glass charm.
5

Step 2: Pick your adventure job

Your job is what you like doing when adventure starts. It gives one bigger class gift that helps the whole table.

6

Adventure jobs

Thistle Knight

Protects friends and stands bravely at the front.

Gift: once per scene, turn a scary moment into a brave one.

Loch Scout

Finds paths, listens for clues, and spots hidden doors.

Gift: ask the Guide one “what do I notice?” question.

Song-Spark Bard

Uses music, jokes, and stories to lift everyone up.

Gift: give another hero +1 after a kind song or cheer.

Hearth Mage

Carries warm, safe magic: sparks, steam, tea, and tiny lights.

Gift: create a small helpful magical effect.

Beast Friend

Understands animals and earns trust with gentle patience.

Gift: ask a friendly creature for a small favor.
7

Bestiary style

Creatures are encounters, friends, mysteries, or puzzles — not bags of hit points. Every entry keeps a picture, a feeling, and a table action together.

8

Creature entry pattern

Art rule: image first, overlay second, never a floating framed box.

Moon-Kelpie Foal

A young water horse with silver mane and shy eyes. It wants someone to find the bell it lost under the reeds.

At the tableUse this
WantsIts moon-bell returned.
Helps byCarrying one hero safely across shallow water.
ComplicationIt splashes when nervous and soaks the map.
Kind solution: sing softly, offer oats, or ask the loch what it remembers.

Guide note

For this age group, a creature should usually have a feeling before it has a fight. Scared, lonely, proud, sleepy, hungry, confused, or protective gives Adventurers something to respond to.
