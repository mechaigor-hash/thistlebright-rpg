Player Handbook — Character creation, HP, MP, leveling, kindreds, jobs, cantrips, spells, gear, and a worked example

Adventures in Alba

Scottish fairy myth, classic fantasy adventure, gentle danger, and table rules made for brave Adventurers.

How to use this book

  1. Choose who helps as the Guide.
  2. Make a hero in five easy steps.
  3. Use Strength, Int, Agility, Wis, or Luck when a roll is exciting.
  4. Keep the story warm: heroes can be muddy, surprised, or silly, but never shamed.
Table feel: point at the picture, ask what the hero notices, then roll only when it is exciting.
For Adventurers 5–7: read choices aloud, let adventurers point to pictures, and use small bonuses (+2, +1, +0) instead of lots of maths.

What you need

  • One six-sided die.
  • A pencil, character sheet, and three little counters.
  • A grown-up or older sibling Guide.
  • A promise to listen and take turns.

What players say

  • “I help.”
  • “I try the brave thing.”
  • “Can I talk to it?”
  • “I look closer.”
  • “Can my friend help?”
First table promise
Every player gets a turn to say one idea before the dice decide anything.

Welcome to the glen

Tiny bells ring beneath the heather. A fairy guide invites the heroes to help a magical place without making the rules too big.

3

The easy dice rule

When the answer is obvious, no roll is needed. When everyone leans forward and wonders what might happen, roll one six-sided die and add the hero's matching stat bonus.

TotalResultSay it like this
1–3WobbleIt goes wrong in a funny, safe, or messy way.
4–5Yes, but...It works, and there is a tiny cost or choice.
6+Bright successIt works well, and the hero feels proud.
No failure spiral: a wobble changes the scene; it does not stop the adventure.

The five classic stats

  • Strength — lifting, climbing, holding, pushing, protecting.
  • Int — puzzles, facts, plans, reading runes, remembering.
  • Agility — sneaking, dodging, catching, balancing, jumping.
  • Wis — feelings, animals, nature, noticing, sensible choices.
  • Luck — charms, surprises, odd magic, last-chance moments.

When not to roll

  • The action is safe and obvious.
  • An adventurer is only describing how they look or feel.
  • The group already found a kind solution.
  • A roll would slow a warm moment.
Helping rule
If another hero clearly helps, add +1 or let the adventurer roll again and choose the better die.

HP, MP, and resting

Heart Points (HP)

HP shows how much bumping, slipping, startling, and tiring a hero can handle before they need a rest. Heroes are never described as badly hurt; low HP means muddy, shaken, sleepy, or overwhelmed.

LevelHero HPHero MP
110 + Strength6 + Wis or Luck
2++2 HP each level+1 MP each level

Magic Points (MP)

MP fuels adventure spells. Cantrips cost 0 MP. Most spells cost 1 MP; bigger story spells cost 2 MP if the Guide allows them.

Resting

  • Snack rest: after a quiet snack, regain 2 HP and 1 MP.
  • Safe camp: after a full rest, regain all HP and MP.
  • At 0 HP: the hero sits out one tense moment, then returns with 1 HP when a friend helps.
Gentle rule: HP is pacing, not punishment. Adventurers should feel worried for the hero, not scared for themselves.

Leveling and feats

LevelWhat changesWhat to say
1Choose kindred, job, stats, cantrip, gear."This is who I am at the start."
2+2 HP, +1 MP, learn one new spell or tool trick."I learned from the road."
3Improve one +0 stat to +1."I practised something hard."
4+2 HP, +1 MP, choose one feat."I have a special talent."
5Improve one +1 stat to +2, learn one spell."I am growing into a hero."
8Choose another feat."My legend gets bigger."

Feats every 4 levels

Stout Heart

+3 HP. Once per session, stand back up with 1 HP after a friend cheers you.

Deep Spark

+2 MP. Your first adventure spell each session costs 0 MP.

Lucky Pocket

Once per session, spend a copper, button, or charm to reroll Luck.

Helpful Friend

When you help another hero, they gain +2 instead of +1.

Storm Runner

At level 4+, ignore one weather trouble per session if you describe a bold dash.

Oath Keeper

At level 4+, when you keep a promise despite danger, regain 1 MP.

Monster Friend

At level 8+, one creature you helped may return once as an ally.

Relic Bearer

At level 8+, carry one extra enchanted item without trouble.

Skills and checks

What is a skill?

A skill is a thing an adventurer knows how to try. Skills do not add extra numbers. They tell the table which stat makes sense and what tools might help.

SkillUsual statExample
Climb / HoldStrengthHold a rope while friends cross.
Riddle / RuneIntRead fairy marks on a gate.
Sneak / DodgeAgilityStep around creaky roots.
Notice / SootheWisCalm a frightened creature.
Charm / ChanceLuckTrust a lucky button at the last second.

How checks work

  • Say what the adventurer tries.
  • Pick the stat that matches the method, not the problem name.
  • Add +1 if gear or a friend clearly helps.
  • Roll d6 + stat bonus only if the answer is exciting.
  • On a wobble, move the scene forward with a cost, noise, mess, or choice.
Trap example: A twig-bell trap can be Agility to step over it, Int to disarm it, Wis to notice it, Strength to hold a branch aside, or Luck to bump it in a harmless way.

Combat and danger

Combat is a tense scene

Combat in Adventures in Alba is not about hurting enemies. It is about protecting friends, dodging trouble, tiring a creature out, breaking a spell, or helping something stop being scary.

  1. The Guide describes danger and feeling.
  2. Each adventurer says one action.
  3. Roll only for exciting actions.
  4. Reduce HP when someone is bumped, tired, scared, tangled, or splashed.
  5. At 0 HP, a hero needs help; a creature stops fighting and can be soothed, tricked, or understood.

Combat actions

ActionStatOn success
Protect a friendStrengthFriend avoids 2 HP of trouble.
Dodge claws, roots, or falling stonesAgilityYou avoid harm and move somewhere useful.
Spot the weak point in a spellIntNext hero gets +1.
Calm or distractWisCreature loses 1 MP or changes feeling.
Use a charmLuckTurn a bad moment into a funny cost.
Damage guide: small bump 1 HP, scary hit or trap 2 HP, boss special 3 HP. Keep descriptions safe: muddy, tangled, dizzy, startled, sleepy.

Example scenario: adventurer view

The Bell-Root Path

You see: a path of soft moss, tiny silver bells tied to roots, and a sleeping fox curled beside a moon-rune door.

You feel: the place is quiet, like it is holding its breath.

Your choices

  • Step carefully between the bells: Agility.
  • Study how the bells are tied: Int.
  • Whisper to the fox and ask what it knows: Wis.
  • Hold the branch up for a friend: Strength.
  • Tap your lucky button and choose the quietest root: Luck.

If things go wrong

  • Wobble: one bell rings and the fox opens one eye.
  • Yes, but: you pass, but your cloak snags a root.
  • Bright success: you reach the moon-rune door and find a silver acorn key.
Adventurer tip: describe the idea first. The stat comes after everyone understands what your hero is doing.
30

Example scenario: adventurer combat

The Bramble Sneak jumps out

A red-capped bramble creature shakes thorns and hisses, “No one takes my shiny bell!” It looks more scared than mean.

Adventurer turns

  • Knight: protect a friend with Strength.
  • Scout: dodge through roots with Agility.
  • Bard: sing a silly rhyme with Wis.
  • Mage: spend 1 MP on Glow-Pebble to show the bell is not stolen.
  • Tinker: use Int to spot the bramble knot holding its cap too tight.

How it ends

When the Bramble Sneak reaches 0 HP, it is not defeated forever. It is tangled, tired, and ready to listen. If the adventurers offer the bell back or promise a new shiny button, it becomes a grumpy guide.

Reward: the Bramble Sneak shows a shortcut under the thorn arch and gives one copper it thought was a magic moon.

Spells, gear, and treasure

Rule: magic helps the story; it does not solve every problem alone.

Safe little spells

  • Glow-pebble: make a small light.
  • Tea-steam: warm cold hands or reveal a breeze.
  • Thistle-tickle: distract a grumpy creature for a moment.
  • Kind whisper: help someone say what they feel.
  • Button-bridge: make a tiny bridge for one small creature.
  • Heather-hush: quiet a noisy room for one careful question.
Spell limit
A spell can help one small problem. Big problems still need friends, ideas, and choices.

Starting gear

  • A snack wrapped in cloth.
  • One useful tool.
  • One pretty charm.
  • One thing your hero drew themselves.
  • A spare ribbon, button, bell, shell, or smooth stone.
  • A tiny notebook for clues or doodles.

Useful tools

LanternSee misty paths.

Making a hero: quick checklist

  1. Name your hero.
  2. Pick a kindred.
  3. Pick an adventure job.
  4. Choose stats: one +2, two +1, two +0.
  5. Pick one gear item and one charm.
  6. Answer: “Who do I want to help?”
Worked example: Rowan Moonbutton is a Selkie-Born Beast Friend. Stats: Wis +2, Agility +1, Luck +1, Strength +0, Int +0. Gear: oat pouch. Charm: sea-glass button. Rowan wants to help nervous animals.
Grown-up tip: do not quiz an Adventurer on rules. Ask what they imagine, then translate that into Strength, Int, Agility, Wis, or Luck.

End of session

Ask: “Who did we help?” “What should we draw?” “What promise did we make?”

Track level

After a finished adventure, mark one story star. Four story stars usually means the hero gains a level.

If a player is stuck

Offer two choices: “Do you want to talk kindly, move quickly, or do the brave thing?”

Earned mounts and pets

Companion rule

Mounts and pets are story friends, not shopping items. Earn one by helping it, keeping a promise, or finishing a quest. A companion never replaces a hero; it opens one extra approach.

  • One companion may actively help the party in a scene.
  • If it clearly helps, add +1 or reveal one clue before a roll.
  • If danger targets it, the Guide offers a rescue choice instead of punishment.
  • A companion needs care: food, rest, kindness, and a job it enjoys.
Earned, not bought: coins can buy tack, feed, or shelter, but trust must be won in play.

More magic: schools and rituals

Four gentle spell schools

Schools are story flavors, not homework. Use them to describe how magic looks, sounds, and asks for promises.

SchoolFeels likeExamples
Hearthwarmth, light, mendingGlow-Pebble, Tiny Mend
Lochmist, water, memoryMist Step, Moon Reflection
Thistleprotection, brambles, courageShield of Thistles, Prickle Path
Storysongs, names, promisesCourage Note, Name Echo

Rituals

A ritual is a slow spell that takes a scene, a promise, and three ingredients. It usually costs 2 MP from the group instead of one hero.

  • Name what you want: light, bridge, truth, calm, memory.
  • Offer three ingredients: coin, feather, song, button, water, ash, berry.
  • Every adventurer says one helpful line.
  • Roll Wis, Int, or Luck if the answer is exciting.
Ritual mishap: on a wobble, the ritual works but leaves a funny sign: purple hair, hiccuping bells, sleepy shoes, or talking moss.

Enchantments and magic items

Enchanting rule

  • Choose a plain item: blade, cloak, ring, harp, lantern, boots, spoon, shield.
  • Choose one enchantment word: Bright, Thorn, Loch, Cairn, Storm, Rowan, Moon, Ember.
  • Pay the ingredient cost and make a promise about how the item will be used.
  • Enchanted items add +1 only when the story clearly matches their word.
Limit: carry one active enchanted item per level. Mythral items count as two.
